The result

ρ* is a theorem, not a measurement

Every instrument has a ceiling. Express the distance from a tail threshold to that ceiling in units of the tail’s own scale and you get one dimensionless headroom, ρ, which puts volts, accelerations and percent on the same axis. Expand the generalised-Pareto log-density about ξ = 0 and the two score equations collapse, at ξ = 0, into a single condition on the censored observable Z = min(Y, ρ), with no free parameters left in it.

E[Z²] = 2 · (E[Z])²

Both moments are elementary, so the critical headroom is the root of a scalar equation. Below it the fitted tail index goes negative and the fit asserts a finite upper endpoint for a quantity that has none.

The scope

Whose failure is this, the instrument’s or the estimator’s?

The tempting claim is that any tail estimator seen through a finite-range instrument breaks this way. It is not true, and the true version is stronger.

Hill can never report a bounded tail. It is a mean of log-ratios of order statistics, so it is non-negative by construction. Under a hard ceiling it returns exactly zero and calls the tail exponential. It is blind to the symptom, not immune to the cause.

Pickands is exactly invariant to any ceiling above the threshold, bit for bit, because its three inputs are ranks k, 2k and 4k and the largest of them is the threshold. It is immune because it discards precisely the data the ceiling corrupts. That is avoidance, not correction.

So the defensible claim is: maximum-likelihood estimation breaks this way, and estimators that structurally discard the corrupted region hide the problem rather than solve it. Stating it that way pre-empts the obvious objection, because it explains why reaching for Pickands is not a fix.

That is three negatives, which is a warning without a remedy. There is a fourth case, and it is the useful one. Tell the likelihood what the instrument did. A sample sitting on the rail is not a measurement of the value; it is the statement that the value was at least the rail, and a censored likelihood says exactly that. At ρ = 2, where the naive fit returns −0.999, the censored fit returns 0.334 against a true 0.3.

So the failure was never in maximum likelihood. It was in a likelihood written for data that had not been clipped. The recommendation is not to change estimator; it is to model the censoring.

And Pickands is accurate here. 0.297 at every headroom. Its weakness is not bias — it is that the same 0.058 error appears whether conditions are good or terrible, because it never looks at the data that would tell it apart.

The trap

Does the failure announce itself?

A law that says an instrument is lying is only useful if you can tell when it is. So the law and the ceiling detector were run against each other, which had not been done. The answer was the wrong one.

At ξ = 0.3, over four-thousand-point series, detection power was 0.00 for every headroom at or below 4 — precisely the region where the naive fit's relative bias runs from −114% to −689% and a fifth of the sample sits exactly on the rail. The most obviously clipped data in the sweep was the data the detector could not see.

Two causes compounded, and both are instructive. The null was simulated from a tail fitted to the series itself, so under heavy censoring it inherited a hard endpoint and reproduced the pile-up it was meant to control for: the null was contaminated by the failure it was testing. And when the pile-up grew large enough, the threshold used to build that null landed on the rail, leaving nothing above it — and that path also returned “no ceiling”.

The fix needs no null. Under any continuous distribution the maximum is almost surely unique, and the density falls in the tail, so the largest value should carry the fewest repeats. A rail inverts that. Ranking the maximum's multiplicity against the distinct values below it takes power to 1.00 across the entire blind region, at a measured false-positive rate of 0.000 — including through an 8-bit converter, which is the case built to fool a test like this.

The scope limit, stated rather than discovered later. The tie test reads raw measurements. Run against this project's own bearing series it finds nothing, because those are RMS and envelope features: averaging destroys the tie signature even when the underlying instrument was clipped. A feature pipeline can hide a rail from the only test that sees it reliably.

“I could not look” is no longer reported as “there is nothing there.” Three paths used to return the same boolean for both. On a project about instruments that give a reassuring wrong answer, that is the same defect one level up.

Put together

Detect, locate, correct — or say you could not

The pieces above existed separately and did not talk to each other. One answered “is there a rail”, another answered “what is the tail given one”, and the caller was left to notice that the second question is only well posed after the first. In practice that means the correction gets applied when somebody remembers to, which is not a property of the data.

One call now does all three, and the third outcome is the one that would not otherwise exist:

  • A ceiling is found — located from the exact ties, which give its position for free and to full precision, then fitted with the censored likelihood.
  • No ceiling is found — fitted naively, and told plainly that not detected is not not there: it means the limit was not exercised in this sample, which is a different claim from the limit being far away.
  • Inconclusive — no tail estimate at all. The detector has regimes where it cannot answer, and returning a confident number there would attach an answer to a question nobody managed to ask. That is the failure this project studies, committed by the analysis rather than by the instrument.

Where it does fit, it reports how much of the estimate is actually supported: below ρ* the correction is carrying the whole thing, and the result is flagged as weakly identified rather than handed over as a number. The trigger is ρ* itself, not a round constant — which is the point of having derived it.

It also had to be taught its own lesson. The first version chose the tail threshold by rank, and with a fifth of the sample on the rail the rank-k statistic is the rail: every excess is zero and it returned “inconclusive” on the most obviously clipped data available. The exact defect it exists to prevent, one level up.

The consequence

The threshold is not free either

Extreme-value practice says to put the threshold as high as the data allows, because the generalised-Pareto approximation improves with height. Under a ceiling that advice spends something nobody was counting. Raising the threshold moves it toward the rail while the tail scale grows beneath it, so the headroom falls on both counts, strictly:

dρ/du = −[σ_u + ξ(L − u)] / σ_u² < 0

There is therefore a highest usable threshold, and it has a closed form that agrees with a numerical root to one part in 10¹³:

u_max = (L − ρ*σ₀ + ρ*ξ u₀) / (1 + ρ*ξ)

Sample size does not rescue it. Half a scale unit above that bound, a fit reports a bounded tail from 29,901 exceedances. The bias is misspecification, not sampling, so collecting more data at a greater height makes it worse.

And the window between this bound and the height the approximation itself requires can be empty. On Student-t with ten degrees of freedom seen through a ceiling six standard deviations up, no threshold is both adequate for the approximation and clear of the rail. That is the practical face of the divergence in the curve above: ρ* grows like ln(1/ξ) as ξ → 0, and a light tail also needs the highest threshold before a Pareto fits it. The two requirements move the same way and close the window together. A near-exponential tail behind a finite-range instrument is the hard case twice over, and the two reasons are not independent.

On the record

Decisions fixed before the hardware exists

No cells have been measured yet. Everything below is validated against simulated traces with known ground truth, which is exactly why it can be settled now: when real cells arrive there should be no design deliberation left, only execution.

Excitation: a whole-period maximum-length sequence
The case is dominance, not magnitude. Against the charge-neutral bipolar square it wins in every feasible design tested. Against a random binary sequence it wins in roughly a third, at a median ratio of 0.99 — that is, it does not win. It is chosen because it is equally efficient while being exactly balanced rather than balanced by luck, and because it deletes the seed from the design. That argument never used the retracted 31×, and it does not use the corrected 3× either. excitation.waveform_justification
Register length: the shortest that resolves the corner
At a matched record length, which is matched bench hours, the short register with a long bit beats the long register with a short bit in 63 of 65 matched pairs, median 1.75× and up to 6.2×. A Warburg response grows as √t, so the bit duration is the lever; the register length only repeats an excitation already chosen. The recorded reasoning picked the register for spectral flatness and treated it as free. It is the dominant cost. excitation.feasible_designs
Cohort: reach the 22-cell coverage step
Distribution-free 90% coverage is a step, not a slope: below 22 cells no method certifies it at any effect size or budget, so 16 and 20 buy nothing. Priced with the chosen design, 22 cells costs 49.7 bench hours at a 0.07 Hz coupling corner, inside a 60-hour budget. The worry that 22 was out of reach was an artefact of pricing the cohort at a register length picked for spectral flatness. Three bits off the register is worth ten cells. Below roughly 0.05 Hz the step is unreachable and the study should certify the level it can and say so. analysis_plan.coverage_step_decision
Futures third leg: a search small enough to finish
Only ρ between about 3 and 10 carries information. Above 10 an exchange limit is never exercised and no amount of history helps. Inverted into something you can act on before requesting any data: a fixed daily price limit worth 3.3 to 7.5 daily standard deviations. Two of the three filters are free and run on public documents. futures_limits.contract_shortlist

Standing limitations

What this cannot currently settle

Selection bias is real and currently unfalsifiable. Every cell shares one provenance, so no contrast exists. Cells salvaged from a failed pack over-represent neighbour-proximity stress and are survivors of it.

The cross-domain claim is two legs and a gap, not three. A bearing clamp and a battery rail are real finite-range instruments. The equity universe is not: a rule-free ceiling detector finds one in 1 of 80 series, fewer than chance would give.

The channel design question is not answerable from healthy cells at any sample size. The noise dependence is measurable on healthy cells; the anomaly’s common-mode fraction is a property of the fault. That is a data-collection requirement, not an analysis choice.
